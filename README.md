# cybersecurity-engineering-notes

Introduction:
--

What is the Security Engineer?
---
A Security Engineer is a technical problem-solver who designs, builds, and continuously tests systems and policies to protect an organization's digital assets and minimize cyber risks.

Why do Organizations Need Security?
---
Organizations need cybersecurity—and the security engineers who build, test, and maintain it—to safely leverage modern technology and protect their digital assets from cyber threats so they can achieve their business goals without disruption.

Qualifications Required for a Security Engineer
---
A Security Engineer is a foundational problem-solver who breaks down complex security challenges using a background in IT operations, networking, operating systems, basic coding, and risk management principles to adapt to unpredictable, evolving threats daily.


Introduction 2:
---

Asset Management & Inventory
---
A security engineer must continuously track and maintain a detailed inventory of all digital assets—including IP addresses, applications, access controls, and owner details—because an organization cannot protect systems it does not know exist.

Security Policies
---
A security engineer creates, enforces, and monitors organization-wide security rules based on core security principles, while carefully evaluating and mitigating risks when business needs require policy exceptions.

Secure by Design
---
A security engineer maximizes security return on investment by embedding protection into the system's foundation from day one, which includes building secure network architectures, hardening operating systems and Active Directory, and enforcing secure software development lifecycles.

Security Assessment & Assurance
---
A security engineer ensures continuous protection against evolving threats by coordinating regular audits, assessments, and red/purple-team exercises, then prioritizing and fixing any vulnerabilities discovered.



Introduction 3:
---

Ensuring Awareness
---
A security engineer periodically conducts targeted awareness training for employees to prevent social engineering attacks and ensure teams follow security best practices, recognizing that humans are often the weakest link in defense.

Managing Risks
---
A security engineer acts as a trusted advisor to management by identifying vulnerabilities, evaluating their likelihood and impact, and applying mitigating controls so the organization can make informed business decisions about accepting or reducing risks.

Change Management
---
A security engineer tracks all infrastructure and application updates within the organization to perform risk assessments and penetration testing, ensuring new changes do not introduce security vulnerabilities.

Vulnerability Management
---
A security engineer continuously monitors systems for newly discovered software flaws and prioritizes patching or applying mitigations based on vulnerability severity.

Compliance and Audits
---
A security engineer works with internal and external auditors to ensure the organization adheres to industry standards (such as ISO27001, SOC2, or PCI-DSS) and maintains its formal security certifications.



Introduction 4:
---

Managing Security Tooling
---
A security engineer evaluates, configures, and fine-tunes specialized tools—such as SIEMs, Firewalls, WAFs, and EDRs—to maintain continuous visibility and defense across the organization's infrastructure.

Tabletop Exercises
---
A security engineer runs simulated incident scenarios—such as a phishing attack or endpoint compromise—to test team playbooks and evaluate how effectively everyone responds during a real cyber incident.

Disaster Recovery and Crisis Management
---
A security engineer contributes to crisis planning and business continuity frameworks to ensure critical IT systems and data can be rapidly restored following a disaster or major security breach.
